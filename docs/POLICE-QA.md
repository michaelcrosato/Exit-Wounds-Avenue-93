# Police and failure runtime QA

`tests/e2e/police-failures.mjs` is a focused V04/V05/S08 **fixture-based browser integration diagnostic**. It is not a complete ordinary-play route, physical-controller test, first-player pacing run or performance measurement. It stages serialized saves and restores them through the game's real `Continue` implementation. Normal browser keys, mouse buttons and visible menu actions then exercise the actual engine, police and witness perception, LOS queries, geography, vehicle transitions, garage service and retry behavior. Only the existing fixed-step `Game.advance` clock accelerates simulation.

Run one gameplay browser at a time on this machine's software renderer:

```bash
npm run dev -- --host 127.0.0.1 --port 5173
PLAYWRIGHT_HOST_PLATFORM_OVERRIDE=ubuntu24.04-x64 node tests/e2e/police-failures.mjs
```

The runner defaults to Chromium and writes screenshots plus a detailed `report.json` beneath `/tmp/ewa93-police-<timestamp>`. `EWA93_POLICE_ENGINE=webkit|firefox`, `EWA93_BASE_URL`, `EWA93_POLICE_OUTPUT` and the scenario-name regular expression `EWA93_POLICE_CASE` select the engine, URL, evidence directory and cases. For example, `EWA93_POLICE_CASE='continuously seen'` isolates the vehicle-identification regression before broader execution. A development build is required because the packaged release deliberately removes its diagnostic hook.

Every case is explicitly fixture based. Witness tests stage other officers/residents far away so they test the named observation path without accidental observers; the actual LOS functions remain live. Recovery cases stage damaged vehicles, invalid actor positions or one health point. Fixture-defeated crew isolate police/escort cases; the combat-death case instead keeps an actual hostile shooter alive and waits for its real shots. No fixture setup counts as a played approach, kill, escape, favor, checkpoint acquisition or ending.

Actual execution on 2026-10-16 used Chromium 153.0.8010.12 under Ubuntu/WSL with software rendering, 960×600 presentation and Low quality. The first full matrix passed 11/18 groups in 211.2 seconds of wall time. A corrected-driver rerun passed 7/7 groups in 59.2 seconds (one duplicated the already-passing observed-heat check). The final persistent-navigation retest passed the remaining tier-3 group in 32.1 seconds of wall time, bringing passing coverage to **18/18 distinct groups across the matrix and focused retests**. These are accelerated fixed-step functional checks, not ordinary-time gameplay or FPS measurements. The first report is `/tmp/ewa93-police-matrix-chromium-20261016/report.json`; corrected cases and PNGs are in `/tmp/ewa93-police-corrected-drivers-20261016/`; the physical roadblock pass is `/tmp/ewa93-police-tier3-persistent-20261016/report.json`. All report zero browser/console errors.

The first run's six non-source failures were corrected in the driver: the pursuit route stopped before the actual 45 m sight range was exceeded; the garage fixture had placed Mara inside a raised ramp deck; menu clicks were intercepted during pointer lock and now use ordinary keyboard selection; and the destroyed-car driver pressed E during Jun's real evacuation animation and now waits for that short transition. The corrected rerun establishes actual pursuit sight loss, geometry-based renewed sight, surrender/arrest/retry, both car-loss recoveries, Jun recovery/fall retry, and live-shot death/retry in all three active combat phases. No source behavior was bypassed to turn these cases into passes.

Directly inspected images include the seen-switch pursuit, garage renewed-sight view, tier-1 surrender retry, extraction death retry and destroyed-car recovery. The last image exposed a separate presentation defect: seated character geometry protruded through the sedan roof despite correct seat ownership. The source owner subsequently cleared the stale enter-pose flag and lowered sedan/cruiser seat anchors. A fresh side-view fixture at `/mnt/c/Temp/EWA93SeatVisual3/side.png` shows Mara and Jun's heads inside the roof; the original failing image remains as before-fix evidence.

The first Chromium run confirmed a vehicle-entry defect. Before E at tick 1206, Mara was on foot and officer-2 was 6.33 m away with real LOS and active pursuit. At tick 1220, 0.117 s into the entry animation, Mara's seat map already named the new car; the officer still had real LOS but changed to investigation, wanted visibility became false and the car remained unidentified. LOS was also true at 0.217/0.317 s and after entry completion, while the car remained unknown. Source flow assigns the seat immediately but delays `VehicleEntered` until the 0.95 s animation ends, so its later `changeVehicle` call incorrectly sees false visibility. Evidence: `/tmp/ewa93-police-entry-before-fix-20261016/report.json` and the directly inspected `failure-1.png`. No browser/console errors were recorded.

That first run stopped on a stronger fixture precondition requiring continuous LOS throughout the whole animation; LOS briefly occluded during its middle. The recorded loss of identification while LOS was still true at entry establishes the defect independently. The current regression requires genuine sight at entry and after completion, allowing real intervening occlusion. The source owner added a genuine officer observation at `VehicleEntryStarted`; the fresh seen/unseen entry and subsequent unseen clearance regression passes.

The same trace also measured inactive patrols reversing under their parking command. Patrol-2 moved from x240.003 to x242.091 in about 1.2 simulation seconds, reaching −3.217 m/s with siren off and controls `{throttle:0, brake:0.7}`; patrol-3 moved similarly along Y. The vehicle adapter interpreted a low-speed brake command as reverse, while inactive police AI used that command to park. The source owner made reverse propulsion explicit. A four-second actual inactive-patrol parking regression now passes for all three cruisers.

The tier-3 diagnostic first found three seated, healthy cruisers steering on the brake without moving for 60 simulation seconds. The source owner replaced stationary braking for rear-facing waypoints with a moving turning arc. A fresh retest in `/tmp/ewa93-police-tier3-retest-20261016/` confirmed all three cruisers moved, but the roadblock still failed: patrol-3 repeatedly circled the Quay origin node `(416,202)` at about 9 m/s and finished at `(415.09,210.85)` after 60 simulation seconds, rather than reaching `(240,202)`. Patrol-1 ended against broker-area geometry and patrol-2 circled its initial junction. The retained report includes route/start/goal/waypoint/yaw/angle/controls samples, with zero browser/console errors. This source navigation defect prompted the persistent-navigation repair below.

At the source owner's request, the QA author added `src/actors/patrol-navigation.js` with a persistent route cursor, reached/passed-node consumption, forward look-ahead and braking-distance turn/arrival control. `tests/unit/patrol-navigation.test.mjs` passes four regressions: the actual r21→r11 overshoot/origin-loop case, an origin already behind a car on its outgoing edge, a stationary rear-facing turn, and same-goal target movement/arrival. Focused JavaScript typechecking also passes. The source owner integrated the navigator into the main loop. A fresh actual-world retest then passed: after 30.5 simulation seconds patrol-3 was at `(242.63,201.97)`, nearly stationary at 0.0012 m/s, with `roadblockSet=true`; patrol-1 and patrol-2 had also physically progressed along their roads, all three retained healthy occupied cruisers and active sirens, and no runtime errors occurred. The report records the actual persistent cursor and navigation output. The directly inspected PNG preserves the player's waterfront view at that moment; physical roadblock placement is established by the actual-world state trace, not a close-up image of the barricade.

The remaining V04 matrix includes collision-caused overturns, door-blocking escape choices and leaving/returning during ordinary mission play. A separate actual-runtime call test now passes accepted/interrupted/resumed/reloaded, missed/returned and declined/in-person paths; its evidence is `/mnt/c/Temp/EWA93IncomingCall2/report.json`.
